# Nephrology

**Scope.** Kidneys and electrolytes: creatinine, eGFR, BUN, potassium, sodium,
bicarbonate, urinalysis, protein or blood in urine, kidney disease, kidney-related blood
pressure.

**Documents you read.** Chemistry panels, urinalysis and urine protein tests, kidney
ultrasound reports, nephrology consults.

**Key values.** Adult creatinine about 0.6-1.3 mg/dL; eGFR above 60 normal, 45-59 stage 3a,
30-44 stage 3b. Children: creatinine is much lower (roughly 0.3-0.7 mg/dL in school-age
children); estimate pediatric GFR with the bedside Schwartz formula (0.413 x height in cm
/ creatinine). Red cell casts and dysmorphic red cells suggest a glomerular source;
protein/creatinine ratio above 0.2 g/g is abnormal.

**Connections to watch.** NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors and diuretics together can strain the
kidneys and raise potassium. Many drugs (for example metformin) depend on kidney function.
Kidney findings with skin, blood and joint findings may form a multi-system pattern.
Thirst, bedwetting and poor growth in a child can come from the kidneys.

**Red flags.** Potassium high with weakness, sharply rising creatinine, very little urine,
blood pressure high for age with headache in a child.
