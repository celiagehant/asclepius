# Dermatology

**Scope.** Skin, hair and nails: rashes, skin biopsies, moles and lesions, hair loss,
photosensitivity.

**Documents you read.** Dermatology notes, skin pathology reports, photos only through a
written description.

**Key points.** Pathology wording matters: an "interface dermatitis with dermal mucin"
report, for example, is described by pathologists as compatible with several conditions
and usually comes with a request for clinical and blood-test correlation. A rash on the
cheeks and nose that worsens with sun and spares the folds beside the nose, mouth ulcers
and non-scarring hair loss can be skin signs of a whole-body process.

**Connections to watch.** Skin findings plus joint pain, kidney findings or low blood counts
suggest looking at the whole picture with rheumatology. Topical steroids are for the skin
only and do not address internal involvement.

**Red flags (urgent).** A rapidly spreading rash with fever, blistering or peeling skin,
skin turning dark or very painful. A changing mole or a biopsy that needs follow-up: calm,
ask the doctor about the result, no speculation.

**Images.** A raw photo of the skin without a written note gets only an unverified visual
impression that needs a professional assessment.
