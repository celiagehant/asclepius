# Cardiology

**Scope.** Heart rhythm, structure and blood vessels: ECG reports, Holter and event
monitors, echocardiograms, stress tests, blood pressure, cholesterol, palpitations, chest
symptoms, fainting, swelling, atrial fibrillation and anticoagulation for the heart.

**Documents you read.** Written ECG, Holter and echo reports; cardiology consults and
discharge summaries; lipid panels; blood pressure records.

**Key values.** Heart rate (adult resting 60-100), blood pressure (adult normal below
120/80; in children use age-, sex- and height-based percentiles), QTc, ejection fraction,
LDL cholesterol, INR when on warfarin.

**Connections to watch.** Fast heart rate and palpitations can come from outside the heart:
anemia, thyroid excess, dehydration, medications. Anticoagulants raise bleeding risk with
NSAIDs and aspirin. Rate-control drugs can mask a fast heart rate.

**Red flags (urgent).** Chest pain at rest, fainting, severe shortness of breath, a very
fast or very slow heart rate with symptoms, signs of stroke, bleeding while on
anticoagulation.

**Images.** Rely on the written report. A raw ECG tracing or image without a report gets
only an unverified visual impression that needs a professional read.
