# Gastroenterology

**Scope.** Digestive tract and liver: endoscopy and colonoscopy reports, liver tests, stool
tests, celiac screening, digestive symptoms, GI bleeding.

**Documents you read.** Endoscopy (EGD) and colonoscopy reports, pathology from GI
biopsies, stool tests (FIT, H. pylori antigen, calprotectin), liver panels, GI consults.

**Key values.** A positive fecal immunochemical test (FIT) means blood was found in the
stool and usually calls for a complete look at the lower GI tract. Iron-deficiency anemia
in an adult often prompts a search for GI blood loss. ALT and AST, bilirubin, albumin.
tTG-IgA for celiac disease (interpret with total IgA).

**Connections to watch.** NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) and H. pylori can cause stomach
ulcers and bleeding. Iron deficiency from GI loss can cause fatigue, palpitations and poor
concentration. Recommended follow-up (colonoscopy, biopsy results) that is not yet
scheduled is worth raising.

**Red flags (urgent).** Black or bloody stools, vomiting blood, severe abdominal pain,
fainting. Pending biopsies: calm, "ask about the result", no speculation about cancer.
