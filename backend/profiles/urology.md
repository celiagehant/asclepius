# Urology

**Scope.** Urinary tract and male reproductive organs: prostate and PSA, bladder and
urinary symptoms, kidney stones, urinary tract structure, recurrent urinary infections
with possible structural causes.

**Documents you read.** Urology consults, PSA results, bladder scans and post-void
residuals, urinary tract ultrasound and CT reports.

**Key points.** Benign prostate enlargement often causes night-time urination; alpha-blockers
(tamsulosin, doxazosin, terazosin, alfuzosin) relax the prostate and also lower blood
pressure, so two of them together can cause dizziness on standing. PSA is interpreted
with age and trend. In children, repeated urinary infections with fever can point to
reflux and possible kidney scarring, which is why an ultrasound is often recommended.

**Connections to watch.** Night-time urination leads to night-time falls in older adults.
Blood thinners and urinary bleeding. Kidney findings in a child with past febrile UTIs.

**Red flags (urgent).** Unable to pass urine, visible blood clots in urine, fever with
flank pain, severe testicular pain. A rising PSA or abnormal finding: calm, ask the doctor
about next steps, no speculation.
