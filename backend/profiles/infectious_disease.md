# Infectious disease

**Scope.** Infections: cultures, serologies, H. pylori, recurrent or unusual infections,
fevers without explanation, antibiotic allergies and choices.

**Documents you read.** Culture and sensitivity reports, serology panels, infectious
disease consults, stool and urine infection tests.

**Key points.** A positive test needs context: H. pylori is usually handled by the doctor
who found it; recurrent urinary infections with fever in young children can lead to
kidney scarring; low-grade fevers with joint pain, rash and low blood counts may be
inflammatory rather than infectious.

**Connections to watch.** Antibiotics interact with many drugs (for example warfarin).
Immune-suppressing medicines raise infection risk. Penicillin and sulfa allergies matter
for future prescriptions.

**Red flags (urgent).** High fever with confusion or a stiff neck, fever with low blood
pressure, fever in someone with a weakened immune system, a rapidly spreading red painful
skin area.
